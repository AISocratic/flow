15Strategic Gantt view

The long-range view of the company, over time.

The Workflow Graph answers what can run next. The Gantt answers where the company is against its year. It covers mission, quarterly goals and KPIs, projects and epics — tasks stay in the Kanban and Workflow Graph views, where they belong, and generally do not appear here at all.

  • Planned and actual dates — start and end, side by side, so slippage is visible rather than inferred.
  • Status and percent complete — on track, at risk, blocked, or complete.
  • Relationships — which projects serve which goal, and which epics sit inside which project.
  • Project-level gates — the epic-to-epic dependencies that decide when a gated epic can start.
  • The metric trend for each goal, beside the bar that claims to move it.
  • Projects that span more than one cycle, drawn crossing the boundary rather than truncated at it.
  • Epics awaiting outcome data — deployed, but not yet judged.

Mission, goals, projects, epics — tasks stay in the Kanban and Workflow Graph views. Percent complete blends task completion, epic evaluation scores, deployment status and observed KPI impact; the Workflow Graph remains the authority on what can actually run.

Percent complete is not card count A project can read as 90% done while having moved nothing. Progress at every level blends task completion with the epic evaluation score, the deployment status and the observed KPI impact — which is why an epic can sit at deployed, awaiting metrics instead of jumping straight to complete.
