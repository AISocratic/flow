17Agent OKRs & performance

Scorecards for the fleet — not company goals for agents.

Agent OKRs are quarterly scorecards used to understand which models, harnesses, agent configurations and execution policies actually perform. Their purpose is routing and accountability across the fleet; they do not give agents independent objectives of their own. An agent identity is the whole configuration — model, version, harness, system prompt, skills, tool permissions, effort level and memory setup — because that is the unit whose performance you can actually act on.

  • Throughput — cards completed, epics contributed to, cycle time.
  • Quality — first-attempt success rate, verification scores, number of rework loops, PR approval versus rejection.
  • Consequences — regressions or incidents introduced, merge reverts, test, lint and build outcomes, code-quality signals where measurable.
  • Cost — token usage and cost per accepted card.
  • Independence — how many human interventions the work required.

KPI impact needs care. A card or epic may contribute to a metric change without being its sole cause, so Flow distinguishes direct attribution, modeled attribution and simple correlation rather than presenting all metric movement as agent-created. Performance then feeds back into the execution-policy router: over time the system learns which configuration is most reliable for migrations, frontend work, debugging, planning, review and content — and routes accordingly. Promotion and demotion are policy, and both are recorded and reversible.
